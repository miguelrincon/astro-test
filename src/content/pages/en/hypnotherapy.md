## What actually happens

Hypnosis is a natural state that most people slip into several times a day — while driving a familiar route, reading a good book, or staring at the sea. You are awake, aware and able to speak. The difference is that your attention narrows and your inner critic quiets down a little.

In a session, I guide you into that state with a slow, spoken induction. Once you are comfortably relaxed, we work with the thoughts, images and sensations connected to the change you want. Often this means revisiting a memory from a safer distance, replacing a frightening image with a steadier one, or rehearsing a response until it feels familiar.

You remain in control the entire time. You can open your eyes, move, speak or stop whenever you like.

## Common myths

**“You will make me do things I do not want to do.”**  
No. Hypnotherapy is collaborative, and you cannot be made to act against your values. You are always the one in charge.

**“I will be asleep or unconscious.”**  
No. Most people describe it as a pleasant, heavy, daydream-like state. You hear what I say and remember the session afterwards.

**“It only works on weak-minded people.”**  
Not at all. It tends to work best when you are curious, motivated and able to focus — qualities that have nothing to do with being suggestible in a childish sense.

**“It is a quick fix.”**  
Hypnotherapy can create a powerful shift, but lasting change usually comes from repeated, gentle practice. We build that into the work.

## What it can help with

There is a growing body of research on hypnosis for pain, anxiety, sleep and habit change. It is not a cure-all, and it is not a replacement for medical or psychological treatment. It is a focused tool that many people find useful alongside other care.

I work most often with anxiety, stress, sleep, confidence, phobias and habits. If I think another approach would serve you better, I will tell you honestly in the intake.
